We are pleased to share the second article in our four-part series with you ahead of its wider release. The series examines how a population with fewer young people and a growing share of older people could reshape labour supply, public services, consumer demand and the public finances, issues we believe will be relevant to your work.

The article considers whether growing political and social differences between young men and women could make relationships harder to form and place further pressure on the United Kingdom’s already low fertility rate.

The political divide is widest among the youngest adults

Economic malaise has been established as a key driver of increased divergence in political and societal views. Studies have linked this polarisation to both stagnating living standards and increasing economic inequality. Most western economies have experienced both over the past few decades.

This has implications for dating and relationships, not only because there is an established link between divorce rates and political affiliation mismatches, but because a large proportion of young people say this is an important aspect of choosing a partner. This goes beyond voting intentions. Young people have notably diverging views on issues such as abortion and gender roles.
